Case Study: NinjaKat Language
Academy — From Traditional
Academy to Autonomous Digital
Operation with AI.
How I transformed a 3-year English academy into a fully automated digital
machine — without hiring anyone. Just one founder, AI, and many hours of
work. While I do have recurring collaborators such as teachers and
consultants, I take pride in saying that this company is now managed by a
single individual who controls more than 20 digital tools in sync.
The Context
NinjaKat Language Academy was born 3 years ago in Chamartín, Madrid, as an in-person English
academy with a different approach: learning through play, without boring textbooks, without
academic rigidity. The challenge was the deeply unequal competition between well-established
traditional academies and those barely breaking into the market. We closed for a period due to lack
of assets and liquidity — but the idea never died, and it continued until it became what it is today. An
in-person academy with a digital soul.
After COVID, one thing became clear: our students loved in-person classes. We decided to
never offer online classes, because we would be sacrificing real interaction and effective learning for
the sake of profit margins. Classes are always physical, no matter what — because at some point
we were all students with unique needs, and we needed teachers who gave everything to education.
We wanted to replicate that: put the student first, acknowledge their individual needs, and above all
make classes interesting. We are currently located at Calle Enrique Larreta 1, Chamartín, Madrid.
As for the name, corporate image, colors, and slogan — I did something no one else does. I let
someone very special to us choose them. Our first student, a 12-year-old boy who is now 16, asked
me what the academy was called. When I didn't have an answer, I asked him: "If you had an
academy, what would you call it?" He shouted: NinjaKat. I then asked him to design the logo, and
with copy-paste in a Google Doc, he brought to life a very angry yellow cat wielding a sword. The
idea then developed on its own, thanks to a future professional and his wild imagination.
What changed: Everything else.
The Challenge: We set out to do everything differently from what traditional academies do —
transforming all administrative and marketing operations into a 100% digital, human-free context, so
we could focus on what truly matters: personalized education.
Without hiring anyone. Just one founder, AI, and 5-6 hours of work daily during the week plus some
full weekends — while also depending on external employment to cover investment needs and
unexpected expenses. We managed to build a fully digital and automated infrastructure from end to
end, with minimum investment, constant innovation, and the use of native tools.
The Problem
Before the transformation, NinjaKat depended on:
- Manual lead management and follow-ups — including endless calls and emails, especially
from people with complaints, questions, and claims, a process that could take an entire day and
full attention.
- Inconsistent social media posts — with no metrics, excessive editing work, keyword and trend
research, and monitoring of interactions and engagement.
- An outdated website with no SEO — plus frequent plugin update issues, some plugins
incompatible with each other, and no specialized WordPress webmaster for immediate fixes.
- No online store — depending on a single revenue stream regardless of good or bad months.
- No automation of any kind — every process required manual confirmation and
implementation.
- The founder's entire time absorbed by repetitive administrative tasks, always running without
time.
Result: No time for what truly matters — teaching English and personally interacting with students.
The Technology Stack Built
AI Agent — "Kat"
This agent — or first employee — was born from the realization that I needed someone to cover
repetitive tasks and basic operations: responding to WhatsApp messages, handling emails from
potential leads, creating social media content, SEO optimization, and much more.
OpenClaw + Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic API) came to save the machinery we already had but
that was severely rusted and disconnected — no communication between body and brain.
OpenClaw was used as the brain of this operation and connected directly with Claude, although we
could have done the same with any other AI available at the time.
I installed it using GitHub's vast library, where all the necessary commands were available to solidly
integrate the entire brain. I then opened my computer's terminal and entered command by
command with Claude's help every time an error appeared. After 3 days, it seemed like I had
managed to automate conversations from my hands through Claude — and Claude translating to
OpenClaw — until everything came to life and Kat was born.
I didn't use a supercomputer like the big engineers in Palo Alto, California do. This incredibly
complex procedure was installed on a secondhand MacBook Pro 2017, macOS Ventura. It's a
complete myth that you need an Nvidia processor or a Mac mini — which can cost between 600 and
1,000 euros — running 24/7 to make this kind of success story work.
The main interface: I manage it through Telegram. I created my first bot using a simple program
called BotFather, already built into Telegram — you just need to search for it. With just the /Start
command, you have thousands of options. I named the bot Kat, as it's both the company mascot
and a very common human name. It turned out to be perfect when the Telegram bot started
communicating with me and others — in the end, it imitates all our natural human qualities.
After around 100 training prompts in Telegram — covering identity, services, pricing, leads,
follow-ups, tone, bookings, sales, objections, specific parameters, and many errors — Kat began
functioning naturally.
I then asked it to set up automatic Cron Jobs: 8am, 12pm, 4pm, 8pm (Madrid time). A Cron Job is
when you want it to do something automatically without being asked — like a checklist to follow.
This way, it checked emails at set times rather than constantly. This saved us two things: tokens by
not using it repeatedly and unnecessarily, and the discomfort of students and contacts receiving
emails or WhatsApp messages at inappropriate hours, causing panic and resentment.
Automated Communication
This was the first repetitive task we wanted to implement at NinjaKat: getting Kat to communicate
automatically, responding to everything without requiring constant intervention. If something unusual
is happening, Kat alerts me so I can pause and correct.
I accessed my hosting (SiteGround) and gave Kat all the credentials — in addition to the email, I
provided the IMAP/SMTP — admin@ninjakat.org, so that every form filled out on the website would
arrive automatically, with only the most useful information extracted. This allowed me to do two
things: gain direct access to future leads, and extract all useful information to feed into the CRM I
use (Fluent) — which handles follow-ups, birthdays, retargeting, reducing academic dropout,
membership renewal reminders, and improving contact with students to organize schedules using
automatic apps and plugins (SimplySchedule).
We use a lead follow-up sequence: day 1, 2, 5, and 7 — automatically contacting leads to confirm
continued interest in enrolling. This is very different from cold calling, where constantly phoning
someone creates desperation and resentment, ultimately losing the lead forever.
Website & E-Commerce
Then came the moment to stop depending solely on English classes — and instead sell
merchandise that could complement the monthly budget and deliver better results. Not just that, but
also give students an identity and a sense of pride in where they study. We also leveraged this to
create "free ambassadors" — people who promote our content, image, and services at no cost.
- WordPress + Elementor — ninjakat.org. Using Elementor's AI, we built the entire visual
identity 3 years ago, then refined it with Kat — especially when we introduced current SEO
keywords, new blog posts, pages, relevant content, and images.
- WooCommerce + Printful — we call it the DojoStore (containing automated merch with 58
products). What we did was connect WooCommerce's API so everything was organized in
columns with prices, descriptions, and a payment button, linked to Printful — a company
specializing in merchandise creation using the dropshipping model. We adopted this model
because it requires minimal intervention. A student can order a NinjaKat t-shirt or cap and have
it delivered to their home without us touching a thing.
- Kat has all customer information and can monitor shipments end-to-end while personalizing
messages with the customer's name.
- Stripe — integrated payments and data collection for taxes and financial statements. Kat can
also view income, costs, and expenses for each operation, enabling weekly and
month-to-month projections.
- WPForms — lead capture, CRM data input, and syncing everything into a spreadsheet.
- Fully optimized SEO: 15 elements, 5 new content pages, 24 planned monthly posts, and
current keywords. We use Keyword Planner through Claude's integration with Supermetrics —
so we can anticipate trends and seasonal changes.
- Summary of our e-commerce automation flow: Successful test purchase — complete flow:
Stripe → Printful → bank → Delivery at home.
Digital Marketing
Perhaps the most important section, because it saved us the excessive cost of hiring a marketing
agency.
- Google Ads — 2 active campaigns: one on Performance Max and one on Search Display.
We invest around 300 euros in each per month, depending on search behavior in our area. We
prioritize students living in neighborhoods near the academy for proximity and ease of
commute. The campaigns include images, Google My Business — where all our information
and reviews are — and we use Google's free advisory service alongside an expert.
- Google Tag Manager — conversions configured with Tag Manager experts to track
conversions and total time invested, extracting key metrics for Google Ads such as CPC, CPA,
CTR, and ROI.
- Supermetrics MCP — real-time campaign analysis and optimization using AI that can view
and intervene in Google Ads. This tool can intervene, pause, and alter any campaign if given
permission — becoming a second brain for NinjaKat, focused purely on marketing.
- Buffer + Metricool — content scheduling for Instagram and TikTok. This can also be
connected via API, but it requires a special paid plan. For this part, we prefer human
intervention as it makes content scheduling easier — while ensuring all copywriting is perfect.
- Meetup — we use this for scheduled in-person events, where we attract leads who come to
visit the academy for free, participate in a game or skill-building session. It has an API, but we
prefer our own copywriting to seem more human. After all, we enjoy these events too.
- Wallapop — for local lead generation. Very few leads come through this channel, but those
that do are nearly pre-sold — ultra-specific inquiries from people seeking urgent help.
Additional Automation and Future Projects
A company interested in accessing the same services and replicating the same results recently
requested configuration of a primary AI agent for their business. "Sara" has been born to meet the
needs of Echo Smart Living, based in Denver, USA. Although it specializes in home and building
construction and renovation, the AI automation model can be replicated equally well. Even though

completely separate cases, the administrative model can be fully replicated — and succeed.

The Real Obstacles — Unfiltered and Honest
Not everything is rosy for the entrepreneur.
1. The learning curve was hard — but with time, mastering this many
tools is possible.
It took me nearly a month, day and night, to learn to operate OpenClaw with internal commands.
Understanding APIs, how they work, and automatic linking took a week. WordPress took me almost
5 years of creating hundreds of websites, thousands of failures, and learning about hundreds of
plugins such as WP Forms, Fluent CRM, WooCommerce, Elementor, and many more. Syncing
Printful with WooCommerce also took hundreds of hours — first creating product templates, building
mockups, setting prices with a profitable margin aligned with the market, writing descriptions, and
publishing them in a logical order. Setting up Stripe for payment automation took a week. Learning
to teach English took nearly 7 years, 3 master's degrees, 1 undergraduate degree, a lot of family
support, and a lot of patience to master many of these tools.
2. The "rate limit" problem
The most frequent error: accumulated sessions from cron jobs exhausting the API context. Tokens
need to be guarded like gold — because in the end, more than 20 euros a month can disappear on
cron jobs and new instructions for Kat. So generating prompts with the fewest characters possible is
very useful. I started using a trick I saw on Reddit: an American suggested talking to these
intelligences like a "caveman," which helped preserve tokens.
But a trick I might want to patent: use ChatGPT to refine prompts first, then pass them to Claude to
be ultra-specific with no room for questions. Filtering two or three times is perhaps what has worked
best for prompt generation.
Technical Limitations
OpenClaw can read WhatsApp but cannot initiate outbound conversations — so "cold calls" are out
of the question, and I would need to hire someone for that. It works only as lead support, not as a
lead generator. I've even had to pause it at times because it becomes invasive — for example, after
an alert of more than 30 messages, I realized it was talking to a potential client about dogs, travel,
and lifestyle. Which, incidentally, also becomes a token problem and a risk of releasing confidential
information — which forced me to add a parameter in OpenClaw to never reveal sensitive
information like passwords.
5. The first Meetup event — only 1 person showed up.
The first Game Night had 1 attendee. Lesson learned: charge a symbolic entry fee (3€) so that
people who sign up feel bad about skipping and wasting money. Also send a reminder 48 hours
before each event about the benefits of learning English. The good news: I automated it, and the
copy is persuasive and friendly. And with the person who did show up, I closed a sale and had a
great conversation.
6. Google Ads without initial conversions
204€ spent without conversions at the start, because Google Ads has a learning curve based on
real interactions and searches. Cause: Performance Max was spending 74% on junk traffic and
useless keywords — for example, people searching for free English classes, which Kat thought
were real leads.
The fix had to be manual: 500 keywords negativized + budget rebalancing using Supermetrics MCP
directly, along with very specific parameters for Kat about the type of audience and keywords I want.
7. OpenClaw does not natively support multiple agents
We tried to create "Sara" for Echo Smart Living with its own Telegram bot, but using the same
neural network as Kat — as if one brain had two personalities. There were two problems: first, it only
supports one bot per installation, which would require a new terminal, new installation, and new
costs. Second, we ran the risk of the two personalities crossing and generating incorrect,
hallucinated information.
Simple solution: Railway with a separate 24/7 instance functioning as an external terminal, using the
same flow as Kat but without any risk of identity crossover.

The Philosophy
"Technology does not replace the teacher. It frees the teacher to teach."
NinjaKat will never offer online classes. Classes are in-person, in Chamartín, with games, events,
and real community. We don't believe in sacrificing personalization to increase margins. We believe
in differentiating, personalizing, and monitoring the learning process.
AI handles everything else — so that the only work that remains is the one that matters: being in
front of the students.
